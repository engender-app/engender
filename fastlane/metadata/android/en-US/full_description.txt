engender helps you understand your gender by giving time and context to your feelings. How masculine or feminine are you feeling today? Any dysphoria? Mark those and anything you want on your own scales. It only takes a moment. After a while, open Look back and see how your gender has changed over time.

engender is made by a trans person for other trans people. Use whichever parts fit your life, whether you're questioning, planning your next step or years into transition. It's free, with no ads, subscriptions or accounts to create.

Your gender, day by day

A mood is enough for an entry. When you have more to say, set your gender scales and add a note, photo or voice recording. Every scale has a name and endpoints you choose. You can write several times a day and use tags to find things later. You can also record how you feel about different parts of your body.

What your entries show

In Look back, choose a stretch of time, like one month or the time since you started HRT. Follow a scale day by day, with a second one beside it if you like, or plot two against each other. You can see each scale's average and the one that moved furthest, and which tags came with a change. engender draws what you entered and leaves the reading to you.

Put photos side by side or open Wrapped for a week, month or year. Browse your calendar or search your notes and tags to find a particular moment. Safe space keeps favourite entries and your own list of things that help within reach on harder days.

Keep your care records together

Track HRT and other medication with dose logs, schedules and reminders. Keep lab results alongside the changes you notice, including hair changes and cycle tracking if you use it. For surgery, keep your plans and recovery notes together. Note referrals and how long you've been on a waiting list, who you see, and what each visit or prescription cost. Before an appointment, gather your questions and prepare a clinician summary to take with you.

engender does not diagnose conditions, interpret lab results, recommend doses or replace medical advice. Consult a qualified healthcare professional about treatment decisions.

The things you want to try and remember

Trying a name, different pronouns or a new look? Note how it feels over time. Record voice practice and compare it with your earlier recordings. Keep track of hair removal and time spent binding or tucking. Mark your milestones, plan your next steps and follow a name change through each office that needs it. Or write yourself a letter to open on a date you choose.

Your journal stays with you

The Android app works offline and has no internet permission. It sends no journal data or usage statistics to a server. You decide when to export or share anything.

Your stored journal and media are encrypted. Some startup settings and key-management metadata remain outside that encryption, and taking a photo can leave a temporary unencrypted camera file before import. The privacy policy explains these limits.

You can unlock with a passphrase, PIN or your phone's biometric or screen lock. Optional disguise mode gives the app a neutral name and icon.

Keep a copy, take it with you

Save a password-encrypted backup or schedule backups to a folder you choose. If another phone saves its backups to the same folder, engender can merge in the newer one when you open it. An archive lets you move your journal to another device or the web app. You can bring in entries from Daylio, too.

CSV, JSON, printable journal books and other readable exports are not encrypted. Anyone with those files can read them. Keep a backup off your device if you want to be able to restore your journal after losing it.

Choose your flag colours, switch between light and dark, and pin the areas you use to Today.
